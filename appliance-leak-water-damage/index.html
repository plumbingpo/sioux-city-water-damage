Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Sioux City

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Sioux City, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the Machine DownStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Document What FailedPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.

Flooring and Subfloor

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Rooms Next Door

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.
