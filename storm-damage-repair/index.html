Signs It Is Time for Storm Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Rooms that start to smell stale or moldy following a storm
  • Flooding in lower levels because drainage systems were overwhelmed
  • Puddles near skylights, beside a chimney or on the attic floor
  • Wet insulation in the attic or wall cavities found after bad weather
  • Water getting past doors, windows and wall penetrations when wind pushes the rain
  • Missing, damaged or lifted shingles you can see from the ground
  • Ceiling drywall that sags or bulges, or paint that bubbles after rain

Why Storm Damage Repair Is Not a DIY Job

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. A roof leak that looks minor from inside may have done extensive hidden damage to insulation, roof decking and wall cavities, and that leads to mold growth and structural deterioration if it is not found and dried. With moisture meters and thermal cameras, trained technicians follow the route water took and find all the areas it reached.

Temporary protection such as tarps and board-up needs correct technique or it will not hold. Tarps put on the wrong way may funnel water into the building or tear loose in the wind. Experienced in storm mitigation, our team picks appropriate methods and materials to keep the building protected until permanent repair work can be scheduled.

How to Prevent Storm Damage Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
  • Schedule a roof inspection every year so weak spots are repaired before storm season
  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so water drains properly in heavy rain
  • Reseal worn caulk and weatherstripping around windows and doors before the rainy season
  • Cut back limbs that hang over or close to the house so wind cannot throw them into it
  • Photograph your property once a year so its condition is on record for insurance purposes
