Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Sioux City Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Signs to Look For

  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Walls You Cannot See

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Insulation Holding Water

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. Drying from inside the room is not enough when the insulation is soaked.

Water Next Door

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.
