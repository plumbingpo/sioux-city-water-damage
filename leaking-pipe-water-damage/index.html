Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Sioux City

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. For Sioux City homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the Water SupplyClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • Years of excessive water pressure straining valves and joints until one of them starts to weep.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Tiny pinholes that form as corrosion gradually works through aging copper pipe.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Damaged Flooring

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Rotted Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Once framing lumber is no longer sound, it must be removed and new wood put in.

Swollen Cabinets

Under a sink, a cabinet base made of particleboard puffs up and falls apart after it takes on water. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.
