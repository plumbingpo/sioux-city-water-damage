Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Sioux City Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Sioux City, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
